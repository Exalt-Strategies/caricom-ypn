---
title: "Why the Caribbean needs its young members organised"
date: 2026-06-05
tag: essay
excerpt: "The region is young, its parliaments are not, and the few young members who win seats too often sit alone. A network is the obvious answer."
---

The Caribbean is one of the youngest regions in the hemisphere, and one of the oldest-governed. Most people are under thirty-five; most of the people writing the laws are not.

When a young person does win a seat, the job can be isolating. They may be the only member under forty in the chamber, learning the mechanics of committees and budgets with few peers who remember doing the same. Good ideas stay stuck in one parliament because there is no easy way to carry them to the next.

A network changes that arithmetic. Bring the young members of fifteen parliaments into one room, and a first-term senator in Belize can compare notes with one in Dominica; a committee reform that works in one chamber can be borrowed by another. None of this requires agreeing on party or ideology. It requires organisation.

That is what the Caribbean Young Parliamentarians Network sets out to build: not a party, not a conference, but a standing home for the region's young elected members. It starts small, with the people who already hold seats, and grows as more come on board.

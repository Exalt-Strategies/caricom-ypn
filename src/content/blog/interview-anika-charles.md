Anika Charles was elected to the Parliament of Trinidad & Tobago at 27. She now
chairs her national chapter of the network. We spoke about the first term,
edited for length.

## On being the youngest by fifteen years

"For about a year I mistook being the youngest for being the least qualified.
Nobody said that to me. The building said it, in a hundred small ways, and I
believed it. The network was the first room where being under thirty was the
normal thing and not the remarkable thing."

## On the first budget cycle

"A budget is where you find out whether you actually understand your job. My
first cycle, a member from Guyana walked me through her notes from the year
before, line by line, on a series of very long phone calls. That is the whole
network in one story. Nobody teaches you that in a campaign."

## On the committee rule

"People think the reserved-seat rule is about fairness. It is really about
information. Committees are where you learn how the money moves. If young
members are never in those rooms, we spend our whole term reacting to decisions
we could have shaped. We stopped waiting to be invited and wrote the rule that
puts us there."

## On what she tells people thinking of running

"Run for the seat, not the title. The fastest way to waste a first term is to
treat it as a stepping stone. Deliver one real thing for the people who sent
you, bring one more young person through behind you, and the rest takes care of
itself."
